# Using personas

As I kept diving into HCI concepts, I realized how useful Personas are, as explained in the Personas chapter from Interaction Design Foundation, for connecting better with users when designing interfaces. Personas are like fictional but super detailed portraits of typical users, based on real data about their needs, behaviors, and goals. In my previous reflection, I talked about how UI and UX are key to making a system functional and enjoyable, but now I see that Personas are like the map that guides you to design an interface that actually makes life easier for the user. In our project, where we’re working on a system to manage medical records, applying Persona knowledge helped us create a prototype that’s more practical and focused on real users.

I chose Alejandra, a therapist in training, because her needs really reflect the challenges faced by the system’s main users. Alejandra is a master’s student with two years of experience in psychological therapy, and her goal is to quickly record and access patient records so she can focus on therapy, not paperwork. Her biggest pain is wasting time searching for physical documents or filling out forms by hand, plus inefficient processes make updating information a hassle. Based on this, we designed a screen in the prototype with an “expediente” section that shows a summary of each patient, with clear tabs like Information, Documents, and Actions. To make it intuitive, we added a “Session Report” option that opens a pre-structured form where Alejandra just fills in key fields and saves everything in seconds. This cuts down her administrative load and gives her more time for patients.

This design came straight from understanding Alejandra as a Persona. The Personas chapter says you should use these profiles to decide which features to prioritize, and that’s exactly what we did. Instead of cramming the interface with a ton of options Alejandra doesn’t need, we focused on keeping it simple and straightforward, with elements that leave no room for ambiguity. This ties back to my previous reflection, because a well-designed UI is useless if it doesn’t improve the overall UX, and Personas ensure both are aligned with what the user wants.
